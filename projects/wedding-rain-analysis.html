Project Overview

The Wedding Day Rainfall Analysis is a personal project inspired by my own wedding planning experience. Having planned an outdoor wedding ceremony, I was acutely aware that rainfall could significantly disrupt our special day. This motivated me to develop a data-driven tool designed to help couples planning outdoor weddings make informed decisions based on historical rainfall patterns. Using 30 years of meteorological data, this project analyzes seasonal trends, geographical variations, and probability distributions to provide actionable insights for wedding planning. The analysis helps couples identify optimal dates and times with the lowest likelihood of precipitation, turning weather uncertainty into a manageable planning factor.

Methodology

Data Collection

Historical rainfall data was collected from the National Weather Service and regional meteorological databases spanning the years 1990-2023. The dataset includes:

  • Daily precipitation amounts
  • Hourly rainfall patterns
  • Weather conditions (cloud cover, humidity, temperature)
  • Geographical data from 12 different regions

Analysis Approach

The analysis followed a multi-step process:

  1. Time series decomposition to identify seasonal trends
  2. Statistical modeling of precipitation probability distributions
  3. Geographical clustering to identify regional patterns
  4. Predictive modeling using historical data to forecast rain probability

Results

Key Findings

  • June through August show the highest variation in rain probability, with afternoon showers being most common
  • Morning ceremonies (8AM-11AM) have 23% lower chance of rainfall interruption across all seasons
  • Geographical analysis revealed 3 distinct "weather zones" with different optimal wedding seasons
  • Rain patterns show strong 7-year cyclical trends correlating with climate oscillations

Conclusions

The Wedding Day Rain Predictor project demonstrates how historical weather data can be leveraged to optimize outdoor event planning. By analyzing decades of rainfall patterns, we've created a framework that helps couples identify optimal wedding dates and times based on statistical probability rather than folklore or guesswork.

Future Developments

  • Integration with climate change models to account for shifting weather patterns
  • Development of a user-friendly web application with location-specific recommendations
  • Extension of the analysis to include other weather factors (wind, temperature, humidity)
